A lion in a dream carries a certain weight. Unlike the sneaking dread of a snake or the swarm of small worries a rat brings, a lion is *presence* — raw power, standing right in front of you. How you feel about it tells you a lot about your own relationship with strength.

## What lion dreams usually mean

The lion is the classic symbol of **personal power, courage, and leadership**. Dreaming of one often reflects your own strength — either strength you're stepping into, or strength you're afraid of and keeping caged. The lion can be the part of you that's ready to lead, roar, or hold your ground.

It also frequently represents **a dominant figure** — a boss, a parent, a partner, anyone whose authority looms large in your life. Whether that lion feels protective or threatening usually mirrors how that power feels to you day to day.

## Common lion dream scenarios

- **A calm or friendly lion** — You're at peace with your own power, or in good standing with an authority figure. Strength that isn't at war with you.
- **A lion attacking you** — A power struggle, a domineering person, or an overwhelming emotion (often anger) you feel unable to control. Sometimes it's your own force turning on you because you've suppressed it.
- **Running from a lion** — You're avoiding your own power or dodging a confrontation with someone strong. The lion keeps pace because the thing you're fleeing is part of you.
- **Taming or befriending a lion** — You're integrating your strength — learning to wield courage and assertiveness without being ruled by them. A genuinely good sign.
- **A lioness** — Often about fierce protection, especially of family or something you love; maternal power and loyalty.
- **A caged or wounded lion** — Power that's been suppressed, dignity that's been diminished, or strength you've talked yourself out of using.

## The feeling matters

Awe or calm suggests you're aligned with your own strength or respect an authority in a healthy way. Fear usually means you feel dominated — by a person, a situation, or an emotion like rage — or that you're intimidated by how powerful you could actually be. That second one catches people off guard: sometimes the scariest thing isn't the lion, it's admitting you *are* the lion.

## What to reflect on

- Where in my life am I being called to be braver or more assertive?
- Is there a dominant person whose power I'm reacting to?
- Am I owning my strength, or keeping it caged out of fear of what it might do?

A lion dream rarely warns of danger. More often it hands you a mirror and asks: *are you ready to stop shrinking from your own power?*

*Dreams are for reflection, not prediction — notice how the lion's strength made you feel.*
