Of all the dreams people search for, this is the tenderest. Dreaming about someone who has died can leave you shaken, comforted, or both — sometimes so vivid you wake reaching for your phone before you remember you can't call them. Whatever it stirred in you, it almost always means something worth sitting with gently.

## What these dreams usually mean

At the most human level, a dream about a deceased loved one is your mind continuing to love and grieve them. Grief doesn't clock out at night. When someone was woven into your days, your sleeping brain keeps them present, replaying their voice, their kitchen, the way they said your name. That's not a malfunction — it's love with nowhere new to go.

These dreams often surface around anniversaries, big life events they're "missing," or seasons when you especially wish you could ask their advice. The dream is your heart filling a gap the day can't.

## The unfinished-words dream

A very common version is the conversation you never got to have. You tell them the thing you didn't say in time; they forgive you, or you forgive them; you say goodbye properly. Dreams like this are the psyche doing repair work — giving you the closure real life didn't. If you wake lighter, that repair landed.

## "Visitation" dreams

Some of these dreams feel categorically different: unusually calm, clear, and warm, with the person appearing healthy and at peace, often offering a simple reassurance — *I'm okay. You're going to be okay.* Across many cultures these are called visitation dreams and are experienced as genuine contact. You don't have to decide what's literally happening to honor how real the comfort is. If it eased something in you, let it.

## The harder versions

Not every dream like this is peaceful. Sometimes the person seems distressed, or you dream of losing them again. This usually mirrors *your* unresolved feelings — guilt, things left unsaid, fear of forgetting them — rather than anything about them. It's a signal to be gentle with your own grief, and sometimes to say out loud (to a friend, a journal, an empty room) the thing still stuck in your chest.

## What to reflect on when you wake

- How did the dream leave you feeling — comforted, unsettled, or both?
- Was there something said, or something you wished had been said?
- Is there a feeling about this person you've been carrying and not expressing?
- What would honoring them look like today, in some small way?

A dream about someone you've lost isn't a riddle to crack. It's a visit with your own love for them — proof that the bond didn't end, it just changed shape. Let the dream give you whatever it came to give.

*Dreams are for reflection and comfort, not prediction — hold onto how this one made you feel.*
